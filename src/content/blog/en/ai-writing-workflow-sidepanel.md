A lot of AI writing happens in one window. Ask, generate, dislike, generate again. It is like leaning on a copier: more paper keeps coming out, and the page you actually need disappears under the pile.

The model is not too slow. **The chat window was never built to finish a piece.** It was built for the next message. Scroll, follow up, open a new thread — it is excellent at that. Gathering a draft, it is not.

## 🪟 What the chat window gets wrong

You have probably hit all of this:

- **Draft and scrap share one river.** The good line, the tangent, the throwaway follow-up — they live in the same thread. Finding the sentence you liked means scrolling up. By the time you find it, you have forgotten why you wanted it.
- **Sources have nowhere to live.** Citations, numbers, someone else's words, what you actually saw — they get trapped in a quote, or they scatter into bookmarks and another doc. Close the chat, and the material scatters with it.
- **The real question gets washed away.** The sentence you were stuck on goes into the box and is gone. The model answers, the window moves down, the question itself is no longer in view. Ask it again in different words, and it is as if you never asked.
- **One window can make a draft sound like a conclusion.** The reply is too complete, too smooth. You nod. Your judgment gets thinner. Generate again, and you are waiting for the model to finish the thinking.
- **Switch models, and the desk disappears.** What you worked through in ChatGPT does not come with you into Claude, Gemini, or Doubao. Each site is an island. Comparing takes copy and paste — and while you copy, the good line has already sunk.

So the mess is often not a lack of effort. The interface rewards “keep chatting.” It does not reward “stop and arrange.”

## 🧺 A sturdier layout: chat on the left, notes on the right

The better setup is boring: **AI page on the left, ThinkNote sidepanel on the right.** Chat stays chat. Arrangement stays arrangement. Sources, questions, and good sentences share one strip of screen.

The sidepanel will not blast one question to every model. It stays put while you switch among ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Doubao, DeepSeek, Yuanbao, Qianwen, and Yiyan — so you can carry the same question across, and later paste the finished draft back. Notes land locally first, then go to the cloud after you pause; close the window or switch tabs, and what you just wrote is usually still there.

Don't wait until the thread ends. While the window is still open, tuck in what the piece needs:

- **Sources**: citations, numbers, the other person's words, what you actually saw. Don't leave them trapped in one model's quote.
- **Questions**: the sentence you are actually stuck on. Write it before you ask. Don't demand a finished draft from the first prompt.
- **Good lines**: the ones that landed — from the model, or from your own mouth. Catch them. Don't let them slip out of the window.

The sidepanel is a pocket, not an archive. Only what this draft can use goes in. A pretty full reply is still raw material. The sentence you would say out loud is yours.

## 🔄 Switch models to confirm, not to make noise

Copy the same question into the first model. Read it through. Mark lines that land or feel off.

Then switch. Paste the same sentence. Listen for disagreement, not for polish. Who filled a blind spot, who invented a detail — that goes in the note.

A single model is good at making a draft sound like a conclusion. A second model shows you different structure, facts that don't match, a tone that is over-certain or hedged. Your job is not to pick a winner. It is to keep the disagreements that matter for **this** piece.

Chase only one to three subtopics you care about. Drop the rest. Attention is scarcer than models.

## ✍️ When the draft is done, paste it back for a last pass

After the sidepanel is in order, write the piece in your own words. That step is yours, not the model's.

Then copy the finished draft out — paste it into the AI pages you were just using, for a last check and polish: facts that don't stand, a tone that is too sure, a line that reads wrong. This time the model is a proofreader, not a ghostwriter.

If it has to become a document, [paste into Word](/en/blog/paste-ai-chat-into-word).

This is the same Chat → Think → Note loop as [How to Upgrade Your Brain with AI](/en/blog/upgrade-your-brain-with-ai), with Note pinned to the right edge — and one extra lap: when you are done writing, let a model look once more.

## 📋 A small checklist

```
Sources, questions, good lines → sidepanel
    → same question to model A
    → same question to model B (confirm, not noise)
    → disagreements and good lines stay in the note
    → write the draft in your own words
    → paste the draft back for check and polish
    → Word only if you need to hand it in
```

When a spark shows up, [note as you chat](/en/blog/note-as-you-chat). For formula-heavy follow-ups, [Learn STEM with AI](/en/blog/learn-stem-with-ai).

Writing software will not finish the thinking. It only stops chat from shoving writing off the screen. Keep chatting on the left. Keep the sentence you would put your name on, on the right. When it is done, send it back for one last look.
